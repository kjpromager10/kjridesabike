Power-Limited Force — the real constraint at speed F = P / v
Max Sustainable Force = P/v vs Speed (km/h) No drag
At any speed v, the maximum force a vehicle can sustain is F = P/v — regardless of wheel torque. This is why wheel torque alone doesn't predict high-speed performance. Two vehicles with equal power produce equal force at equal speed. High wheel torque only matters during low-speed acceleration from rest. Beyond ~60–80 km/h, it's almost entirely a power story. The Torque Number Illusion — Interactive Demo
📢 What happens when you only change the gear ratio?
Move the slider. The motor doesn't change. The power doesn't change. Only the reduction ratio changes — and the "advertised wheel torque" climbs to whatever you want.
The correct takeaway: Wheel torque is real and matters — it's the actual force multiplier at the tyre contact patch. Motor torque is also real. Engine torque is also real. The mistake is comparing numbers measured at different drivetrain points without context. Increasing reduction ratio gives more wheel torque AND less top speed — there is no free lunch. Power is the physical ceiling that cannot be gamed with gearing.

⚠ Full Assumptions & Limitations
  • No aerodynamic drag modelled. Drag force = ½ρCdAv². For a motorcycle at 100 km/h, this is ~100–150 N — comparable to available tractive force. All acceleration values above ~60 km/h are significantly overstated.
  • No rolling resistance. Typically ~0.013–0.02 × mg for road tyres. Small at low speed, non-trivial at high speed.
  • Drivetrain efficiency fixed at 0.92. Real efficiency varies with RPM, load, temperature, and lubrication. Chain drives achieve ~96–98% at optimal conditions; this model uses a conservative combined-losses value.
  • No tyre slip modelled. In 1st gear, calculated forces almost certainly exceed traction limits on most surfaces (~0.8–1.0 × mg for dry road). Real motorcycles wheelie or spin the tyre before reaching these forces.
  • Rider mass excluded. A 70 kg rider on a 144 kg bike increases total mass by ~49% → reduces acceleration proportionally. This is the single biggest real-world correction to these numbers.
  • ICE torque curve is a mathematical approximation. Real dyno curves vary with ride mode (Sport vs Urban vs Rain reduce peak output by ~10%), intake resonance, exhaust tuning, and fuelling artefacts not captured by a smooth spline.
  • EV model is an idealised PMSM. Real motors derate under thermal load, exhibit voltage sag at high RPM, and have current limits that round off the torque-speed transition. Battery state-of-charge also affects peak power.
  • The Generic EV is hypothetical. Engineered to match published performance class characteristics without representing any specific product.
  • Top speed estimates ignore drag. Real top speed = speed where tractive force equals drag force. RPM-limited top speed (shown here) is always higher than real top speed. TVS claims 114 km/h — consistent with aero-drag limiting at ~9,290 RPM in 5th gear.
  • Primary drive included in ICE ratio. Total ICE ratio = G_primary (3.095) × G_gear × G_final (3.461) × η(0.92). All three stages are included in every calculation.
  • Apache RTR 160 4V is 5-speed. The G6 button mirrors G5. No sixth gear exists — top speed is achieved in 5th.
