Physics model: While braking, cooling uses real τ (no scaling) so heat accumulates correctly. When not braking, cooling is 8× faster for playability. Standstill soak is ΔT-driven (k·(T−T_amb)) — heat conducts from hot rotor back through pad at a rate proportional to temperature excess, equilibrating at ~330°C. On a slope, effective clamp force = F_clamp + mg·sinθ, consistently applied to both heat generation and deceleration.

Ceramic 400°C580°C Good Low
Sintered 550°C800°C Moderate High

Why sintered isn't "always better": abrasive friction needs activation heat, causes more rotor wear, and is noisier. For city riding (<200°C rotor temps), organic is the right choice. Match the pad to the operating temperature range, not the maximum capability. Sources: EBC Technical Blog, Di Cocco et al. 2023, Chan & Stachowiak 2004, Limpert (1999).

⚠ Educational Disclaimer

This is an idealized simulation for educational purposes only.

Formulas used (lumped capacitance, linear h(v), piecewise μ(T)) are simplified engineering models, not replacements for full FEA or empirical brake testing. Real-world rotor temperatures depend on caliper design, pad geometry, rotor ventilation, bedding condition, ambient humidity, altitude, and rider technique — none of which are captured here.

μ(T) curves are illustrative trends derived from published studies. Actual friction coefficients are compound-specific, proprietary, and vary batch-to-batch. Do not use this simulator to make braking safety decisions, pad selection for motorsport, or brake system design choices.

Physics parameters (M_R, A_R, F_CLAMP, h(v)) are representative of a 150–200 kg sport motorcycle front brake. Your specific setup will differ.

Sources cited are peer-reviewed or industry-standard references. Model ±15% on absolute temperature. Cooling deliberately accelerated 8× when not braking for playability.
