Model · Sources · Assumptions

CORE EQUATION

Q_net = Q_gen − Q_rej
ΔT = Q_net / (m · Cₚ) · Δt

Heat generation Q_gen scales with throttle position and RPM. At idle the engine produces ~1.2–1.4 kW of waste heat; at WOT this rises to ~18–22 kW depending on mode. Only ~25–30% of fuel energy does work; the rest is rejected as heat.

HEAT REJECTION — AIR/OIL

Q_rej = h(v) · A · (T_engine − T_amb) + Q_oil

Convection coefficient h starts at 28 W/(m²·K) at zero airflow and rises linearly with combined vehicle + wind speed. The oil cooler adds a secondary path that becomes significant only above ~40 km/h. Below that, rejection is severely limited — which is why the RE 650 heats up fast at standstill.

HEAT REJECTION — LIQUID

Q_rej = η_therm · h_rad(v) · A_rad · ΔT + Q_fan

The thermostat acts as a modulating valve: closed below 80°C (engine warms fast), linearly opening between 80–95°C, fully open above. Radiator effectiveness depends on airspeed. The electric fan adds ~800 W of forced convection when temp hits 100°C.

THERMAL INERTIA
Engine mass (~8–9.5 kg effective thermal mass × Cₚ) means temperature cannot jump instantly — this produces the smooth lag you see in real bikes. Noise is added via a first-order filtered random process to mimic combustion pulse variation.

Engine massAir/Oil: ~8 kg steel block; Liquid: ~9.5 kg (heavier with coolant jacket). Lumped-mass model — no spatial temp gradient.
Cₚ (specific heat)Embedded in mass constant. Real steel ~490 J/(kg·K); effective value includes oil thermal buffer for air-cooled model.
Heat generationLinear interpolation between idle and WOT. Real curve is non-linear (VE, ignition timing, AFR). WOT heat ≈ (1−η_th) × fuel_power, assumed η_th ≈ 28%.
Convection h valuesApproximated from literature. Real h for air-cooled fins: 20–80 W/(m²·K) depending on geometry. Radiator values are higher due to turbulent flow.
Fin area (air-cooled)0.35 m² estimated for RE 648cc parallel twin fins. Actual geometry-dependent value not published by RE.
Oil coolerModelled as fixed secondary rejection path. Real oil coolers have their own thermostat bypass at cold temps — simplified here to linear speed scaling.
Coolant pumpFlow rate linearly scaled with RPM (0.002–0.009 m³/s). Real pumps have cavitation limits and pressure-relief valves — not modelled.
RadiatorUniform effectiveness assumed. No modelling of coolant side heat capacity or pipe thermal lag. Thermostat hysteresis not modelled.
Speed/AirflowVehicle speed and wind speed are additive for airflow — valid for headwind scenarios. Crosswind, ram-air duct effects not modelled.
Time scalingSimulation runs at real-time (1s sim = ~1s wall clock). Actual warm-up on RE 650 takes ~5–8 min to reach 70°C — sim compresses this to ~60–90 seconds for interactivity.

WHAT IS THIS?

The oscilloscope shows a simulated throttle signal with dynamically generated noise. This is not real sensor data from any motorcycle. It is a physics-inspired noise model to demonstrate how throttle inputs behave in an embedded system context.

NOISE MODEL

signal(t) = throttle + A·sin(2π·f₁·t) + B·ξ(t)
ξ(t) = low-pass filtered white noise

Two components: (1) a deterministic oscillation whose frequency scales with RPM — mimicking combustion pulse coupling into the TPS wiring — and (2) a stochastic term filtered through a first-order IIR to model sensor noise and vibration pickup.

AMPLITUDE SCALING
Noise amplitude increases with throttle (more vibration at higher load) and decreases slightly with speed (gyroscopic stabilisation, smoother chassis). At idle, noise is dominated by the combustion frequency component (~25 Hz base for a parallel twin at 1400 RPM, i.e. ~23 Hz firing frequency).

WHY IT MATTERS
Real ECUs use low-pass filters and sample averaging on TPS signals to reject this noise. The raw signal you see here would cause throttle hunting if fed directly to a fuelling map — which is why firmware signal conditioning matters.
