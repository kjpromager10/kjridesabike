Below ~3500 rpm, the ECU applies aggressive torque limiters + lean fueling. The underlying torque is available, but the ride-by-wire mapping chops delivery — causing the surging/jerky feel at city speeds.

Stock map shows aggressive non-linear response at low throttle openings.
At 1000–3000 rpm, the stock map delivers ~60–70% of torque at only 30% throttle, then cuts back under sustained low-load cruising. This hunt-and-cut cycle is what riders feel as a surge/lurch. An ECU flash linearises this — 100% gain at 25% throttle position across the range.

Engineering basis

Engineering

Convective cooling — h ∝ v^0.7

Standard forced convection correlation for external airflow. Used to illustrate that cooling effectiveness drops significantly at low vehicle speeds vs highway speeds. Simplified model — actual difference in real conditions may be more extreme.

Standard heat transfer correlation (Churchill & Bernstein / flat plate approximation). Not RS 457-specific — directionally correct, not a precise figure.

Engineering

Oil viscosity drop with temperature → reduced lubrication

As oil temperature rises, viscosity drops. Below minimum viscosity, the oil film between moving parts becomes too thin to prevent metal-to-metal contact, accelerating wear. Well-established lubrication engineering principle.

General tribology / lubrication engineering — not RS 457-specific. The causal chain (heat → viscosity → wear → tensioner failure) is the most likely mechanism but has not been confirmed by an official teardown report.
