What This Is

  • EDU This is an educational visualizer built to help people understand how a 4-stroke engine works — the physics of combustion, valve timing, ignition advance, and variable valve actuation. It is not a simulation tool, not an engineering reference, and not affiliated with any manufacturer.
  • EDU All numbers, curves, and animations are derived from publicly available engineering textbooks, SAE papers, and manufacturer press releases — studied and reconstructed for learning purposes. No proprietary ECU maps, no OEM software, no confidential data was used or reverse-engineered.
  • EDU The engine modeled is loosely based on a 155cc class single-cylinder 4-stroke sportbike (Bore ~58mm, Stroke ~58.7mm, CR ~11.6:1) — used as a relatable real-world example. All parameters are approximations for illustration, not spec-sheet values.

What's Simplified (and Why That's Fine for Learning)

  • GEOM Piston motion uses standard slider-crank geometry (λ = 0.3). Real con-rod ratios vary 0.25–0.33 — close enough that the visual motion is accurate.
  • TIMING Ignition advance is modeled as a simple RPM-only curve (8° at idle → 38° at high RPM). Real ECU maps are 2D lookup tables (RPM × throttle load) and are OEM-proprietary. The curve shown is a textbook representative shape.
  • VVA VVA crossover shown at 7,400 RPM — confirmed per Yamaha R15 V3 spec and service manual. The switch is shown as instant; in reality it's a mechanical engagement via a pin in the rocker arm. Low cam: intake duration ~220°, exhaust ~230°. High cam: intake ~260°, exhaust ~270° — longer duration at high RPM allows better cylinder filling. The R15 does NOT have VVT (continuous cam phasing) — valve timing is fixed; only lift changes at the crossover point.
  • TIMING Valve events use realistic 4-stroke timing: exhaust opens ~490° (50° BBDC of power stroke), intake opens ~700° (20° BTDC of intake TDC — overlap zone), intake closes ~200° (20° ABDC of intake stroke). These match published valve timing conventions for a high-revving sportbike engine (Heywood, Blair). Ignition advance is an RPM-only approximation; real ECU maps are 2D load-RPM tables.
  • VE Volumetric efficiency is a hand-fitted empirical curve matching the general shape of published dyno data. Real VE comes from 1D gas dynamics simulation (Ricardo WAVE, GT-Power, etc.).
  • POWER Torque peaks ~14.7 Nm at ~7,500 RPM; power peaks ~18–19 kW at ~10,000 RPM — consistent with publicly available spec data for this engine class. P = T × 2πN/60. These are crankshaft figures; wheel power is lower after drivetrain losses.
  • KNOCK Knock simulation is illustrative only — it shows the concept of uncontrolled autoignition and timing retard as a general engine concept. The Yamaha R15 V3 does not have a knock sensor. Knock is prevented by design: Yamaha engineers bake a conservative ignition advance map into the factory ECU, pre-calculated per RPM and load. There is no real-time knock detection or ECU retard on the R15.
  • AIRFLOW Airflow arrows are qualitative direction indicators. Real intake port flow involves 3D turbulence, tumble ratio, and pressure wave dynamics not modeled here.

  • TEXT Blair, G.P. — Design and Simulation of Four-Stroke Engines. SAE International, 1999. — Source for valve timing conventions and gas dynamics.
  • TEXT Stone, R. — Introduction to Internal Combustion Engines, 4th ed. Palgrave Macmillan, 2012. — Used for Otto-cycle approximations and piston kinematics.
  • SAE SAE Paper 2004-01-1452 — "Variable Valve Timing and Lift Strategies for High-Speed Engines." — Basis for VVA crossover RPM behavior and lift profile differences.
  • SAE SAE Paper 890388 — "Combustion Chamber Design for High Speed 4-Stroke Engines." — Reference for combustion chamber geometry conventions.
  • PAPER Fontana, G. et al. — "Variable Valve Timing for Fuel Economy Improvement in a Small Spark-Ignition Engine." Applied Energy, 2009. — Supporting reference for VVT phase modeling.
  • OEM Yamaha Motor Co. public press releases and spec sheets for the R15 V4 / YZF-R15 — used only for publicly disclosed figures (VVA crossover range, bore/stroke, power output). yamaha-motor.com

Things Worth Knowing

  • NOTE At 14,000 RPM each degree of crank rotation takes ~12 microseconds. That's why ignition fires 30–38° before TDC — flame propagation time is near-constant, but the available crank window keeps shrinking.
  • NOTE Mean piston speed at 14,000 RPM ≈ 27.5 m/s. Most engines start losing ring seal and hitting flow limits above ~25 m/s — this is why max RPM isn't just about cam springs and materials; it's a fundamental fluid dynamics constraint.
  • NOTE VVA (discrete cam switching) and VVT (continuously variable phasing) are different things. The R15 uses VVA — two physical cam profiles, one engagement mechanism. Honda's VTEC is the same concept. BMW and Toyota use continuous VVT on both cams independently.
  • NOTE Ram tuning — intake tract pressure waves arriving at the valve at just the right moment — can push VE above 100% in a narrow RPM band. This is why race engines have tuned intake lengths. Not modeled here, but it's real.
